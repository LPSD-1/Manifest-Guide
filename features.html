How to read this

This page is the complete list. Every line is something the apps do today — nothing here is planned, in progress, or a thing we mean to get to. Where a line needs explaining, it links to the page that explains it.

Two apps. Manifest is free, goes on every engineer's phone, and there is no limit on how many people run it. Manifest Studio is the one you pay for, once, on the phone or tablet that builds the catalogue. See the two apps.

Everything below works with no signal. The only lines that need a connection are the ones that send something somewhere, and they wait until there is one.

Manifest — finding a part

  • Search every field your catalogue defines, not just a part number
  • Search by voice
  • Scan a QR code or barcode to jump straight to the part
  • Check a code — scan to confirm you picked the right one
  • Browse by any axis you choose: maker, size, rating, material, what it fits, where it lives
  • Filters and sorting that stay put as you move around
  • Every part's own QR code, on screen, to show or to scan from another phone

Manifest — the part page

  • Every field you defined, in your words and your order
  • Where it is kept — rack, shelf, drawer, van — and a pin on the floor plan
  • Photographs, datasheets and documents
  • What goes into it, and what it is used in
  • Private notes and favourites, which never leave that handset
  • Tap a tag to see everything that shares it
  • Share a part with somebody

Manifest — floor plans and assemblies

  • Open site drawings and floor plans
  • Zones and bays, colour-coded, with a count on each
  • See the exact bay or shelf a part sits in
  • Kits and assemblies with quantities, nested as deep as you need
  • What to collect — a pick list for a whole assembly

Manifest — jobs on site

  • Job sheets your office designed, filled in on site
  • Text, numbers, dates, durations, choices, tags, addresses, phone numbers
  • Photographs and signatures taken on the glass
  • A running timer
  • Parts picked from the catalogue — tap one to open it
  • A status the job moves through, in your words — and if you say which moves are allowed, the app permits those and no others
  • Templates for the jobs you do over and over
  • Job history, and a seven-day bin for anything deleted
  • Finished jobs sent to your own systems when signal returns
  • Jobs fetched in from your systems, if you configure it
  • Jobs moved by file between handsets

Manifest — labels

  • Preview and save label sheets for shelves and bins
  • Sized to stock you already buy — Avery, Brother, Dymo, Zebra, plain paper, continuous roll and tape

Studio — building the catalogue

  • You decide what a part is — write the fields and the app takes its shape from them
  • Choices from a list, flat or nested so one sits under another
  • Mark any field searchable, filterable, or an axis people browse by
  • Your own words — Jobs can be Tasks, Plans can be Sites
  • Your logo and colours
  • Sections and categories of your own
  • Six worked examples to start from and cut down
  • Retype a field later without losing what is already in it
  • A seven-day bin for deleted parts

The full list of field types is in the reference.

Studio — getting your parts in

  • CSV and Excel import with a column-mapping step
  • The app proposes a type for each column from what it sees in the data; you can override any of them
  • Merge a second spreadsheet into an existing catalogue without doubling it
  • Bulk edit and bulk tag across the whole catalogue
  • Add parts by hand, one at a time

Studio — floor plans

  • Draw your aisles, bays and zones and give them colours
  • Put a part on the drawing and drag its pin to where it actually is
  • As many plans as you have buildings

Studio — designing the job sheet

  • Design the form: which fields, in what order, which are required
  • Define the states a job may move between
  • Templates for your common jobs
  • Where finished jobs go — HTTPS, email or a folder — with your own field mapping
  • Where jobs come from, if you send work out to handsets
  • Retention and anonymising rules
  • A picking order, so a pick list comes out in the order somebody walks the racking

Studio — getting it onto handsets

  • Pair a handset by scanning a code
  • Sealed catalogues — encrypted, and they open only on handsets you paired
  • A catalogue altered on its way is refused
  • Update a catalogue the handsets already hold — it updates in place, it does not add a second copy
  • A catalogue server you host, so handsets fetch updates themselves
  • Plain export for your own records
  • Re-key the fleet if a handset goes missing
  • Copy job settings to another device

Studio — not losing any of it

  • A recovery file — a lost handset is not a lost catalogue
  • Scheduled automatic backups
  • Reminders when the fleet changes and the backup no longer knows about it

Both apps, everywhere

  • Works with no signal at all
  • Encrypted on the handset, with the key in Android's hardware keystore
  • An optional requirement that the handset has a screen lock
  • Dark mode, large text, and TalkBack throughout
  • No account, no sign-in, and no server of ours — nothing you do reaches us

The two add-ons

Both are one-time purchases and both work on any paid tier, including the cheapest.

  • Catalogue server — a small program you run on your own network so handsets fetch catalogue updates without anybody handing each one a file
  • Service reports — a finished job as a one-page PDF to leave with the customer

And what it does not do

A list this long is worth less if it does not say where it stops. Reading and writing NFC tags, exporting to CSV, printing straight to a printer rather than saving a file, and sending a catalogue phone-to-phone are all things this app does not do today.

What it deliberately is not covers the larger scope decisions — there is no CRM, no stock ledger and no accounts behind this.
