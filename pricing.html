Price and scope

Price and scope

What it costs, what each tier includes, and what the app deliberately does not do.

On this page

  • One payment, no subscription
  • More handsets than ten
  • The two add-ons
  • What it deliberately is not

One payment, no subscription

You buy it once and it keeps working — offline, in five years, and if we are hit by a bus. There is no renewal, no seat count to true up and nothing that stops working because a card expired.

Every paid tier carries the full feature set. What changes is how many handsets you can pair. The two add-ons are extras on top, and both can be bought on any paid tier.

Play has no upgrade path for a one-time purchase, so moving from Solo to Team later means paying for Team rather than the difference. Worth knowing before you choose — and it is why the handset packs below add to what you have rather than replacing it.

The handset app is free. You pay once, for Studio, on the single phone that authors the catalogue. Every handset you pair runs the free app; the tier is what says how many you may pair.

More handsets than ten

Company pairs ten. Past that you buy handset packs, and they add up: a ten and a five is fifteen more, on top of the ten the tier already gives you.

They start at one handset, so you never buy capacity you do not need. Taking on a single apprentice costs one handset, not a jump to the next size up.

The bigger the pack, the less each handset in it costs — from £15 for a single down to £10 each in the largest. The curve never inverts: a bigger pack is always the better buy per handset, so two smaller ones never beat the right one. If you want fifty more, a +40 and a +10 is £520, and it is the cheapest route there.

They add rather than replace because a one-time purchase has no upgrade path. A bigger licence would mean paying the whole new price every time you grew; a pack means you only ever pay for the handsets you are adding.

You can buy the same size more than once. Buy a ten today and a ten is still there to buy next year. Each one is bought once and kept for good — it comes back with you on a new phone, or after a reset, for ever. That is a deliberate trade: a pack you could buy over and over would have to be the kind of purchase Google forgets the moment it is used, and your handsets would vanish with it the next time you reinstalled.

Packs are offered on Company only. Below the top tier a pack costs about what the next tier costs and grants the same handsets, so buying one there would be the worse deal of the two — the app offers the tier instead.

The two add-ons

The catalogue server is a small, ready-to-run program you host on your own network so handsets fetch catalogue updates without somebody handing each one a file.

Service reports turn a finished job into a one-page PDF for the customer to keep — what was done, by whom, when, with the values recorded on the form.

Both are bought separately, on any paid tier, and both are one-time purchases like everything else. Neither is needed to use the app; they are there when the way you work needs them.

What it deliberately is not

Being straight about this saves everybody a wasted evaluation.

NotWhy
Stock controlQuantities are commercially sensitive and go stale the moment they leave the system that owns them
Scheduling or dispatchSeeing the work assigned to you and recording that it was done — yes. Planning who goes where next week — no
A sync engineEach device holds its own copy. Nothing silently merges behind your back
Multi-user with accountsThere are no user accounts to administer. A device is the unit of identity
An editor for everyoneAuthoring is Studio's job, so the catalogue survives contact with a busy Friday
